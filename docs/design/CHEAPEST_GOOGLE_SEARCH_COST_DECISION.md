## Three required recommendations

### 1. Cheapest Google-authorized (first-party) option

**Gemini API Grounding with Google Search** — $0/month at this volume (inside
the 5,000 free grounded-prompts/month allowance on an eligible paid-tier
Gemini project), no browser automation, no corporate-IP exposure to Google.
Caveat, agreed by all three agents: it returns a synthesized answer with
citation URLs/titles, not a raw ordered list of organic results — a materially
different data shape from "top N Google results," and it requires a
billing-enabled Google Cloud/AI Studio project to unlock the free tier (not a
truly account-free path, contrary to a literal reading of "no account
required" — a project/API key is still needed, just not a *consumer Google
account* tied to search).

### 2. Cheapest option that returns raw Google-organic-like title+URL/rank

**SerpApi** — $0/month at this volume (inside its 250/month recurring free
plan), returns an explicit ranked `organic_results[]` array with `title` and
`link` per result — the closest data shape to what Sprint 1 actually asks for.
Zenserp and Serper.dev are equally $0/month at this volume and structurally
similar; SerpApi is favored as primary because its free quota (250/month) has
the widest safety margin over the ~8-10/month need and its documented schema
most directly matches "organic title + destination URL."

### 3. Recommended Job Hunter production choice

**Adopt SerpApi's free tier as the Sprint 1 search mechanism**, with Serper.dev
or Zenserp as documented fallbacks (same cost, same data shape) if SerpApi's
terms or reliability prove unsuitable after a bounded evaluation. This is cheap
($0/month at current volume), returns the exact data shape the business need
asks for, and requires no Product Owner spend approval to start evaluating.
It is **not** literal browser SERP reproduction, so it requires the Product
Owner to amend `SPRINT_1.md`'s wording (see JH-SUP-0016 decision doc) — that
amendment is a prerequisite for calling this Sprint 1 "done," not merely an
implementation detail to route around. Pursue Google WSS partner contact in
parallel as a longer-term first-party path, but do not block on it: no
self-service access exists today, and its real cost is unconfirmed.

## Account and Incognito-vs-Playwright answers (both agents agree)

**No Google account is required or recommended.** The Product Owner's own
observation — Incognito search works with no login — is correct and is not
evidence against automation being the detected factor; account state and
automation-detectability are independent. Adding a dedicated Google account
for this task only adds account-suspension/credential-management risk without
addressing the actual cause.

**Why Incognito succeeds and Playwright is challenged**: not one single flag,
but a stack of simultaneous differences — Playwright's non-persistent,
history-less, cookie-less context; its "Chrome for Testing" build rather than
branded consumer Chrome; automation-oriented default launch flags
(`--no-sandbox`, `--remote-debugging-pipe`, `--disable-background-networking`,
etc., confirmed directly from Playwright's own source in the JH-SUP-0015
forensic pass); a virtual Xvfb display inside Docker; and zero real
human-interaction signal before the one request. A real Incognito session in
an installed branded Chrome has none of these differences even though it is
also "private." Google's exact decision weighting remains unobservable from
outside (per JH-SUP-0015's reconciliation) — this is the best-evidence
explanation, not a proven single cause.
