# JH-SUP-0016 — Independent Google Search Best-Practice Research (Codex)

**Research date:** 2026-09-03. This is an independent assessment made without reading `docs/design/CLAUDE_GOOGLE_SEARCH_BEST_PRACTICE.md` and without making a Google Search request. Vendor and Google product/pricing pages were researched through normal documentation/web research only.

## Executive conclusion

For the business requirement—about two automated runs per week returning current, Google-derived organic-result titles and destination URLs—the cheapest good path is a commercial SERP-data API on its **standing free tier**, initially **serpstack** (100 Google searches/month, $0). It is sufficient for 8–10 searches/month, supports location targeting, and keeps all Google-facing collection off the corporate public IP. Its free plan needs an account/API key; confirm the required Hungary/language parameters in a non-production evaluation before adoption. SerpApi (250/month) and Zenserp (50/month) are stronger alternatives at the same $0 monthly invoice.

This accepts the normal contractual/availability risk of a third-party SERP-data vendor. It does **not** mean the vendor is an official Google API or that its collection method is endorsed by Google. Procurement/legal should approve the chosen vendor and its data-use terms before production.

The separate requirement to literally reproduce a consumer `google.com` browser SERP is not a sound unattended-production requirement. It remains challenged and unsupported in the observed environment. If literal Google results are mandatory, amend the requirement to an approved API contract; the new Google Web Search Service (WSS) API is technically close but currently has no official public price or self-service terms and appears partner-oriented.

## Evidence for price, capability, and the WSS distinction

* serpstack publishes its free 100-search monthly quota, says one API request is one SERP lookup, and describes proxy/proprietary scraping; it also says its Google coverage includes web results. [serpstack pricing and FAQ](https://serpstack.com/pricing/)
* SerpApi currently publishes 250 free searches/month, and its Google organic response schema explicitly carries title and destination link. [SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing) and [organic-result schema](https://serpapi.com/organic-results)
* Zenserp currently publishes 50 free searches/month and a $49.99 smallest paid plan. [Zenserp pricing](https://zenserp.com/pricing-plans/)
* SearchAPI.io currently publishes $40/month for 10,000 searches, location geotargeting, and no charge for failed requests. [SearchAPI.io pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing)
* Serper’s own pricing page could not be rendered by the research service; its public API catalogue reports the 2,500-credit trial and $50/50,000-credit starter pack. Treat those figures as **vendor-attributed secondary evidence** and reconfirm at checkout. [Serper API catalogue](https://github.com/api-evangelist/serper/blob/main/README.md)
* Google’s current Gemini price page says 5,000 free Google Search *requests* monthly on paid tier and $14/1,000 thereafter, and importantly says one customer request may cause one or more Google searches. [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)
* Google now documents WSS as `websearchservice.googleapis.com`; it returns title and full display URL, accepts language/region restrictions, but the same official documentation contains **no price, free allowance, public signup, or $30,000 term**. It requires a partner-format client ID. [WSS search method](https://developers.google.com/web-search-service/docs/reference/rest/v1/TopLevel/search)

The $15 CPM/$30,000-minimum claim is therefore not “current official published WSS pricing.” It is a **reported planned commercial term**, reproduced in a community post apparently quoting an email about a future enterprise service. It may be accurate, but Google’s official WSS public pages do not confirm it. Do not budget, procure, or state it as contractual fact without a Google partner quote. [Reported email excerpt](https://www.reddit.com/r/googlecloud/comments/1qilhrg/is_googles_custom_search_api_being_discontinued/)

## Gemini grounding: useful, but not a SERP replacement

Gemini can cite actual URLs/titles: its response places `url_citation` annotations on spans in model-generated text, carrying `url` and `title`. But Google says the model itself decides whether to search and may generate one or multiple queries; the returned `google_search_result` contains rendering suggestions, while the primary product is a synthesized answer with citations. [Google grounding workflow and response fields](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search)

That makes citations reliable enough to expose *sources used for an answer*, not to guarantee ten ranked organic Google results for the caller’s one query. It can omit sources, reorder them by narrative relevance, perform multiple underlying searches (affecting quota), or decide no search is needed. Use it only after a Product Owner amendment such as: “return current Google-grounded answer and its source URLs/titles”; do not present it as a normal Google SERP adapter.

## Why Incognito human search can work while headed Playwright is challenged

The evidence does **not** prove which Google signal decided the challenge. It does prove one cold, on-prem request was challenged despite a genuine Hungarian ISP corporate IP; no retries, proxy, stealth code, or headless launch were used. The reconciled forensic finding is that IP/NAT reputation and a combined automation/session decision remain live possibilities, with neither provable as the sole cause. [Forensic reconciliation](../forensics/GOOGLE_CHALLENGE_CAUSE_RECONCILIATION.md)

The meaningful contrast is a human’s stock browser and a test harness—not simply “headed vs. headless.” Gate 0 launched Playwright Chromium 151 (“Chrome for Testing”) with a fresh non-persistent cookie/history-less context under Docker Xvfb. The documented launch path had automation/test-oriented defaults including a temporary user-data directory, `--remote-debugging-pipe`, `--no-sandbox`, `--no-startup-window`, disabled extensions/background networking/first-run/sync, and no normal browser profile. The script then navigated directly once. A human Incognito session is also private/cookie-light, but ordinarily comes from an installed stock Chrome GUI, a normal OS display and interaction context, and is not controlled by the Playwright protocol/test launch configuration. Those observable environment/session differences can be scored alongside network history by an anti-abuse system. The evidence did not capture a particular JavaScript, TLS, or CDP detector value, so asserting any one as the cause would overclaim. [Gate 0 source](../../apps/google-browser-search/gate0-onprem.mjs) and [on-prem result](../evidence/SPRINT1_ONPREM_DOCKER_GATE0.md)

## Account and stock-Chrome-worker answers

**A Google account is not required for normal Google Search.** The Product Owner’s successful human Incognito observation is direct evidence. Logging in may add cookies, identity and account reputation, but it does not confer a published right to automate consumer Google Search, cannot be relied on to remove a challenge, and concentrates consequences in the account. A dedicated account is therefore **not** a recommended solution to the automation problem; it merely adds account suspension/security and governance risk.

A real stock Chrome GUI with a managed persistent profile can be a legitimate human-assistance or attended-RPA architecture—e.g., an employee uses Chrome and the system consumes an explicit export/approved interface. It is not legitimate to call it “stealth” or to use it to defeat anti-bot controls. If an unattended worker controls that Chrome through CDP, WebDriver, or any automation framework, automation-control signals and anomalous task/session behavior can still exist; the stock binary/profile removes some test-environment differences, not the fact of automation or Google’s discretion to challenge it. Persistent profile state also introduces credential storage, profile locking, consent/CAPTCHA interruptions, patching, and recovery/monitoring duties. Given the existing challenge and no authorized controlled tests, it is not reliable enough to recommend for unattended production Google SERP extraction.

## Recommendation and implementation boundary

Adopt the business requirement as **“current Google-derived title + destination URL results via an approved data API”**, not literal browser rendering. Subject to Product Owner procurement approval, evaluate serpstack Free first using a single provider adapter: request Hungarian location/language, normalize the first 10 organic `{rank,title,url}` records, log provider request IDs and quota, and fail closed (no browser fallback). Keep SerpApi Free as the equivalent-cost fallback if quality or terms are better. Make no Google browser navigation, no CAPTCHA handling, no stealth/fingerprint work, no proxy rotation, and no paid purchase until approved.

If the Product Owner instead requires an official Google contract and literal result-list semantics, ask Google about WSS partner eligibility and written price; do not assume the reported $15 CPM/$30,000 floor is confirmed. If the owner can accept answer-plus-citations rather than a SERP list, Gemini Grounding is the lowest-cost official Google API experiment, with strict output validation.
